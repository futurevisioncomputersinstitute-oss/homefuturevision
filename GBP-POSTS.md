# Google Business Profile posts — 12 weeks

Every fee, duration and level below is taken from the course pages on the site,
so a post never promises something the page contradicts. Where a course fee is
unclear in our own data it is left out rather than guessed.

## How to post one

1. Open [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and sign in.
2. Top left, pick the branch — **Citylight**, **Vesu** or **Pal**. Each branch
   is a separate profile and a post on one does not appear on the others.
3. Click **Add update** (sometimes shown as **Promote** → **Add update**).
4. Paste the text. Add a photo. Set the button as noted.
5. Click **Post**.

Repeat for the other two branches. Same text is fine — these are separate
listings and Google does not treat them as duplicates.

## Rules worth keeping

- **Posts expire after 7 days.** One a week per branch is the minimum that
  keeps the profile looking alive.
- **A photo roughly doubles engagement.** Real photos of the classroom, a
  student's screen, a whiteboard. Never a stock image.
- **1,500 character limit**, but only the first ~100 show before "Read more".
  Put the point in the first sentence.
- **Never put the phone number in the post text.** It is already on the
  profile, and Google sometimes reads a number in the body as spam.
- **Add the button**, don't write "call us". Each post below names which one.

You do not need all three on day one. Most paid design work in Surat needs

## After week 12

Start again at week 1. Twelve weeks is long enough that nobody notices, and
Google treats a re-post as new. Swap the photo each time.

Better: replace any post that produced no calls with a question a student
actually asked you that week. Those are always the best posts, because they
are the questions other people are also typing into Google.

## What to watch

In the profile, **Performance** shows calls, direction requests and website
clicks. Look monthly, not weekly — single weeks are noise.

The number worth watching is **direction requests**. Someone asking Google how
to drive to you is much closer to enrolling than someone who clicked a link.
