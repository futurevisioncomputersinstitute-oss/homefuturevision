Graphic design courses: types, syllabus and how to choose one

What the different courses actually teach, which software to start with, how long each takes, and how to tell a good course from a badly built one.

Written by Future Vision Computer Institute, Surat · Updated 31 August 2026

The short answer. Graphic design courses come in four types: single-software courses (4–8 weeks), foundation courses covering design principles plus Photoshop and Illustrator (about 4 months), advanced professional courses adding branding, packaging, motion and portfolio work (about 9 months), and college degrees (1–4 years). For design work rather than an academic qualification, a foundation course followed by an advanced one is the usual route. Learn Photoshop first, then Illustrator.

The four types of graphic design course

TypeLengthCoversBest for
Single software4–8 weeksOne tool, menus and workflowSomeone who already designs and needs a specific tool
Foundation~4 monthsDesign principles, typography, colour, Photoshop, IllustratorComplete beginners starting a design career
Advanced / professional~9 monthsBranding, packaging, motion, advertising, full portfolioPeople who want to be employable as designers
Degree / diploma1–4 yearsTheory, history, broad studio practiceAcademic route, teaching, some corporate roles

The gap that traps most beginners is between the first two. A course that teaches only software produces someone who can operate Photoshop but cannot explain why a layout works. Employers notice the difference immediately, because it shows in the portfolio.

What a proper syllabus covers

If a course does not cover all of the following, it is a software course wearing a design course's name:

  • Design principles — balance, hierarchy, contrast, alignment, repetition, white space
  • Typography — classification, pairing, spacing, tracking, when to use what
  • Colour — colour theory, and the difference between RGB for screen and CMYK for print
  • Layout and composition — grids, margins, visual flow
  • Adobe Photoshop — raster editing, retouching, compositing, masking
  • Adobe Illustrator — vector drawing, the pen tool, logo construction, scalable artwork
  • Production — file formats, resolution, bleed, print preparation, export for digital
  • Portfolio — ten to fifteen finished pieces you can talk through

Which software to learn first

Photoshop, then Illustrator. Photoshop deals with photographs and raster images and gives quick, visible results, which matters when you are starting and need to stay motivated. Illustrator deals with vectors — logos, icons, anything that has to scale from a business card to a hoarding without going soft — and it is the tool most design jobs actually list.

Canva is worth learning alongside for fast social media work and client mock-ups. It is not a replacement for either: it cannot produce print-ready vector artwork, and no studio hires on Canva alone.

CorelDRAW or Illustrator?

They are close substitutes — both are vector design tools doing substantially the same job. Illustrator has wider industry adoption, appears in more job listings, and sits inside the Adobe set that studios already run. CorelDRAW is still common in Indian print and signage shops. If you are picking one now, Illustrator opens more doors, and the concepts transfer to CorelDRAW in days if a job needs it.

How to judge a course before you pay

  1. Ask to see student portfolios, not the institute's own marketing work. Finished student pieces tell you what the course actually produces.
  2. Ask how many hours of class time you get, not just the number of months.
  3. Ask whether design principles are taught or only software.
  4. Ask who teaches it and what they have designed.
  5. Ask for fees in writing, with what is and is not included.
  6. Ask whether you leave with a portfolio. If the answer is vague, you will leave without one.

Do you need a degree?

No. Graphic design hiring is portfolio-led — studios ask to see the work and how you got there. A degree helps for teaching and some corporate roles, but for agency, freelance and in-house design work, ten to fifteen strong finished pieces matter more than any certificate.

Common questions

What are the main types of graphic design courses?

Four groups. Software courses teach one tool in four to eight weeks. Foundation courses teach design thinking alongside two core tools over two to four months. Advanced or professional courses add branding, packaging, motion and portfolio work over six to twelve months. Degree and diploma programmes run one to four years at a college. Most people who want design work rather than a degree are best served by a foundation course followed by an advanced one.

What is covered in a graphic design course syllabus?

Design principles such as balance, hierarchy, contrast and alignment; typography, including pairing and spacing; colour theory and colour modes for print and screen; layout and composition; raster editing in Adobe Photoshop; vector drawing in Adobe Illustrator; file preparation for print and digital; and a portfolio of finished pieces. Courses that only teach software menus and skip design principles produce operators rather than designers.

Which software should I learn first for graphic design?

Adobe Photoshop first, then Adobe Illustrator. Photoshop handles photographs and raster images and gives quick visible results, which keeps early learning motivating. Illustrator handles vector work such as logos, icons and anything that must scale without losing quality, and it is the tool most design jobs actually require. Canva is worth adding for fast social media work, but it is not a substitute for either.

How long does a graphic design course take?

A single software course takes four to eight weeks. A foundation course covering design principles with Photoshop and Illustrator takes about four months. A full professional course including branding, packaging, motion graphics and a portfolio takes about nine months. Is CorelDRAW or Illustrator better to learn?

Both are vector design tools and they do substantially the same job. Adobe Illustrator has wider industry adoption, more job listings ask for it, and it integrates with Photoshop and the rest of the Adobe tools that studios already use. CorelDRAW remains common in some print and signage businesses, particularly in India. If you are choosing one to learn now, Illustrator opens more doors, and the skills transfer to CorelDRAW quickly because the underlying concepts are the same.

Do I need a degree to work as a graphic designer?

No. Graphic design hiring is portfolio-led. Studios and agencies ask to see finished work and how you arrived at it, not a certificate. A degree helps in some corporate roles and is required for teaching, but for agency work, freelance work and in-house design roles a strong portfolio of ten to fifteen real pieces matters more than any qualification.

Can I learn graphic design without any drawing skill?

Yes. Graphic design is about arranging type, image, colour and space to communicate something clearly. It is a decision-making craft rather than a drawing craft. Illustration is a separate specialism that does need drawing skill, but most commercial design work, including logos, packaging, social media, brochures and advertising, requires none.
